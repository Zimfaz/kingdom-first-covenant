# Kingdom First Covenant: behavioural review, round 1

Behavioural scientist, 23 Nov 2026. Brief: covenant/docs/brief-2026-11-23.md (questions 1, 4, 5 and 7, plus the three standing questions).

Nothing is built yet, so this reviews the brief and the owner's decisions, not screens. Research is marked **[verified]** (checked today, link in Sources) or **[from knowledge]** (I know the work but did not re-check it today; check before quoting it in public). No figures are invented.

---

## Verdict

The idea is sound, and it goes against how most dating apps make money. Mainstream apps do well when people keep browsing. This app does well when people leave because they are married. Every design choice should follow from that. Offer few introductions, one at a time, at a steady pace. Give each stage a clear "continue or close kindly" decision. Keep photos behind words. Put family and friends around the couple. The research is on the owner's side. Too much choice makes people reject more and feel less satisfied. Attraction grows as people get to know each other. Approval from friends and family goes with relationships that last. The biggest behavioural risks are not streaks (those are easy to rule out). They are (a) photo reveal quietly becoming the real decision point, (b) chaperones being used for control or family pressure, (c) people without a willing chaperone being shut out, (d) "marriage, not dating" turning into pressure to rush, and (e) a monthly subscription that earns most from the people who stay single longest. Fix those in the design before the prototype.

## The three questions

### 1. What is the purpose of the app?

**In one sentence:** to introduce Christian adults, one person at a time, to someone who shares their faith and their hopes for marriage, so that with family, friends and church around them they can decide well and marry.

**Its job in a person's week:** small and occasional, not daily. Once a week or so there may be a new introduction to read and pray over. When a conversation is open, there are a few unhurried exchanges a week with chaperones present. Now and then there is a decision: carry on, meet in person, or close kindly. A good week can mean not opening the app at all.

**Gaps between purpose and likely design (no screens yet, so these are risks to design out):**
- **Dating-app words pull towards dating-app habits.** "Match", "tier" and "profile" suggest a catalogue. Use "introduction" for what the app offers, and "getting to know each other" or "conversation" for what follows.
- **Tier 2 can swallow Tier 1.** If photos arrive straight after a theology match, the first real judgement will be on looks, and the theology becomes a filter people tick through.
- **"Marriage, not dating" can mean rushing.** The purpose is marrying well, not marrying fast. The app has to slow people down as much as it moves them on.
- **The business model can fight the purpose.** A monthly subscription earns most from people who stay unmatched longest. Behaviourally this is the deepest gap (see Questions for the owner).

### 2. What benefit does a person get, even if it is never stated?

| Hidden benefit | Which feature delivers it | Strength of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Being taken seriously.** Everyone here wants marriage, so there is less guessing and fewer mixed signals. | Intent promise at sign-up; the same purpose line on every introduction | Moderate. Clear shared goals reduce ambiguity (well-established in goal research, from knowledge). Not tested on Christian matchmaking. |
| **Less decision fatigue.** No browsing and no "is someone better out there?" | Few introductions, one conversation at a time | Good. Larger choice sets lead to more rejection and lower satisfaction in online dating (Pronk & Denissen 2020 [verified]; D'Angelo & Toma 2017 [from knowledge]). |
| **Knowing your own convictions.** Answering the theology and marriage questions makes people put words to what they believe about children, church, money and roles. | Tier 1 questions | Plausible, not proven for apps. Premarital programmes that discuss these topics go with better marriages (Stanley et al. 2006 [from knowledge]), but that is a different setting. |
| **Being held.** Family, a friend or a pastor walks with you. | Chaperones | Good for the general point: approval from one's social network goes with relationships that last (Sprecher & Felmlee 1992 [from knowledge]; Sinclair, Hood & Wright 2014 [verified]). |
| **Safety and dignity**, especially for women. Less harassment, fewer explicit messages, fewer romance scams. | Chaperones, verification, no private messages | Moderate. Scams rely on isolating the victim, and UK fraud advice is to tell someone you trust (from knowledge). Not tested on chaperoned apps. |
| **Relief from being judged on looks first.** | Words before photos | Good. As people get to know each other, they agree less on who is attractive (Hunt, Eastwick & Finkel 2015 [verified]). |
| **Closure instead of ghosting.** | Kind endings; introductions that close on their own | Moderate. Being ghosted goes with hurt and rumination (LeFebvre et al. 2019; Navarro et al. 2020 [from knowledge]). |
| **Discernment as a spiritual practice**, not shopping. | Decision points; "pray over this introduction" | Pastoral rather than empirical. The pastoral reviewer should check the wording. |
| **Single life lived well while waiting.** | "Pause introductions"; honest pacing | Weak to moderate. The point is to avoid harm: fear of being single goes with settling for less responsive partners (Spielmann et al. 2013 [verified]). |

**Unstated costs**
- **Exposure.** Religion, marital history and photos are special category or sensitive data. Divorced people especially will worry who sees what.
- **Waiting can hurt.** Weeks with no introduction feel like a verdict on you.
- **Asking someone to chaperone** can feel embarrassing, can cause friction in the family, or may be impossible for people who are estranged, new to faith or from abusive families.
- **Family pressure.** The chaperone can become a voice that pushes or blocks.
- **Time and money**, and a sense of failure if months pass.
- **Comparison** with friends who marry, and with "success stories" if the app pushes them.

### 3. How does the app create a feel-good factor?

There is no daily rhythm here, so I've walked through the stages of a season instead.

| Moment | Healthy feeling (aim for this) | Where it can turn |
|---|---|---|
| **Signing up** | Calm and purpose. "I'm doing this properly." Competence, because the questions are clear and "Not sure" is allowed. | The theology questions feel like an exam. Scrupulous people and people new to faith fear they are getting them "wrong". |
| **Waiting** | Hope with patience: a known, steady rhythm and honest words about small numbers. | Unpredictable arrivals bring checking, and checking brings anxiety. "No one yet" reads as "no one wants me". |
| **An introduction arrives** | Curiosity and being seen for what you believe. Gratitude. | A percentage score ("92% match") feels like a grade and promises a precision nobody has. |
| **Conversation with chaperones** | Being held, belonging, the safety to be honest about the big things. | Performing for an audience, or a parent's view crowding out your own. |
| **Photo reveal** | Warmth: a face for someone you already know a little. | Appearance anxiety, the first snap judgement, and the hurt of being closed straight after the reveal. |
| **Meeting in person** | Proper nervous excitement, and safety. | Pressure to "decide by the second meeting". |
| **A kind ending** | Sad, but with dignity. "They were honest and kind." | Shame if any reason is given or implied ("after seeing your photos"). Grief if a new introduction comes straight away. |
| **Engagement** | Joy, gratitude to God, celebration with family. | Being pressed to share publicly, or the app presenting engagement as its "win" and your scorecard. |
| **A long quiet season** | Singleness held as good and complete in itself. Rest. | Loss-aversion nudges ("you'll lose your place"), age or "time is running out" messages, invitations to widen your convictions. |
| **Sunday** | Quiet. Nothing arrives. | Introductions on Sunday get read in church. |

**Healthy feel-good:** meaning, being held, gratitude, the competence of having decided well, the dignity of honest endings.
**Hollow or risky feel-good:** the rush of a new arrival, attention counts (likes, views, "people interested"), compatibility scores, being "chosen" as a reward.

## Brief Q1: making "marriage, not dating" real in the design

1. **Intent promise, short and in the first person.** For example: "I'm here to find a husband or wife. I'll be honest, unhurried and kind, and I'll say clearly when it isn't right." People keep promises they have made themselves more than rules they have been given (commitment and consistency, from knowledge). The app must **never quote it back as a rebuke** ("You promised to…"). Pastoral reviewer to word it.
2. **One conversation at a time.** Allow at most one open chaperoned conversation, plus at most one introduction waiting for your answer. Choice overload works against commitment, and one at a time makes the purpose visible in how the app works. (Owner decision: one, or two at most.)
3. **Introductions, not a feed.** A small number arrive on a fixed day each week (never Sunday). There is no browsing, no search and no "see more". If there is no introduction this week, the app says so plainly and kindly.
4. **Double-blind yes.** Each person privately says "I'd like to be introduced" or "Not for me". A conversation opens only on two yeses. Otherwise both see the same words, "This introduction has closed", so neither ever knows who said no.
5. **Decision points ("decide, don't slide").** Couples who drift into commitments do worse than those who decide on purpose (Stanley, Rhoades & Markman 2006 [from knowledge]). Stages: Introduction, then Conversation with chaperones, then Photos, then Meeting in person (with family or a friend aware), then Getting to know each other with intent, then Marriage preparation with your church, then Engaged. At each step both people privately choose **Continue / I need more time / Close kindly**. Only a joint "Continue" moves things on. Neither person ever sees the other's "I need more time".
6. **Timelines as guides, never countdowns.** Use "Many couples meet in person after a few weeks of conversation." Never use "Expires in", timers or "Day 12". If a conversation goes quiet for about two weeks, each person privately gets one line: "Would you like to keep going, or close this kindly?" Never "You've gone quiet". The same applies the other way: the app never suggests engagement. Marriage preparation is offered as a step, not a test.
7. **Kind endings.** Either person can close at any time in one step, without giving a reason. They choose closing words from a short set the pastoral reviewer has checked (for example, "Thank you for your honesty and your time. I don't believe we're meant to marry. May God bless you."). The other person sees only those words. Nothing says when it happened or why. The closed person never reappears. After an ending, new introductions pause for a short while unless the person asks for them sooner ("Take some time? We'll wait until you're ready.").
8. **No limbo.** An introduction with no answer closes on its own after a set time, with the same neutral words. Silence never becomes a slow rejection.

## Brief Q4: photos without looks-first behaviour

**Evidence.** At a first meeting, looks dominate attraction (Walster et al. 1966, the "computer dance" [from knowledge]). People's stated preferences barely predict who they will actually desire (Joel, Eastwick & Finkel 2017 [verified]). As people get to know each other, their judgements of attractiveness spread out and become more personal (Hunt, Eastwick & Finkel 2015 [verified]). Finkel et al. 2012 [verified] warn that browsing profiles turns people into products and that no algorithm has been shown to predict lasting compatibility. So **delay is the right tool**, but a very long delay brings its own problem: shock or a feeling of deception at the first meeting.

**Proposal**
- **When:** not at the introduction. After the conversation has started, either person can ask to share photos. **Both say yes, and both see at once.** It is mutual, simultaneous and never one-sided. Photos must be shared before meeting in person.
- **How many:** up to three, everyday, face clearly visible. No filters or face-editing. Modesty guidance in plain words, and every photo moderated.
- **Verification selfie:** a live check matched to the photos, kept private and never shown. One quiet line: "Photos checked."
- **No looks signals anywhere:** no rating, no "attractiveness" or "desirability" score in the matching, and no ranking by how many people said yes to someone. Popularity-based ranking was reported at mainstream apps (from knowledge; press reports, not verified). Ruling it out is a design rule for the engineers, not only the UI.
- **A pause after the reveal:** the decision buttons come back the next day, with one line: "Take a day. First impressions grow." Owner decision on whether to make it a firm pause. It respects autonomy less, but it protects people from snap judgements.
- **Endings after a reveal look identical to any other ending.** They carry no timing clues.
- **Who is vulnerable:** people with eating disorders or poor body image, and people with visible disabilities or differences. Swipe-app use goes with more distress (Holtzhausen et al. 2020 [verified], a cross-sectional study, so it doesn't prove cause), and Tinder use with poorer body image (Strubel & Petrie 2017 [from knowledge]). Write no wording about bodies, weight or "type".

## Brief Q5: how chaperones change behaviour

**Good**
- Network approval predicts stability (above). Family and friends see things the couple can't.
- Slower, more considered messages, less sexual pressure, less harassment, harder scams.
- It fits the traditions' sense that marriage belongs to the community, not just the two people.
- A real sense of being held, especially for women and for people who have been hurt before.

**Bad**
- **An audience changes what people say.** People present themselves for whoever is watching (from knowledge, Goffman; "context collapse", Marwick & boyd 2011). Honest talk about divorce, health, fertility or the past may shrink, and that is exactly the talk marriage needs.
- **Control and coercion.** A parent-chaperone can push someone towards a person or block them. At worst, this enables forced marriage, which is a criminal offence in England and Wales ([gov.uk, verified]).
- **Exclusion.** People with no willing or safe chaperone (estranged, new to faith, converts from another religion, abuse survivors, care leavers, older widowed people) are shut out.
- **Stalling.** A slow or absent chaperone stops the conversation. That brings frustration, then workarounds: swapping numbers early, which takes the conversation outside the app's safety.
- **Privacy of the other person.** Their words and later their photos are seen by people they didn't choose. Both sides must consent to each other's chaperones.

**Design to keep the good and limit the bad**
- **A table of four.** Each person brings one chaperone (two at most). Everyone sees everyone's name and role ("Anna's friend", "David's parish priest").
- **Chaperones can read and write, but cannot approve, veto or close for anyone.** The decisions belong to the two adults alone.
- **A short chaperone promise:** "I'm here to support, not to decide. I'll keep what I read private."
- **Change your chaperone at any time, privately.** The others see only "Anna's chaperone has changed."
- **A private line to the app that no chaperone or match can see:** "I feel pressured" or "I'm worried about my safety". It leads to help (999, the Forced Marriage Unit, Samaritans) and to an urgent review by the owner. **This is the most important safeguard in the whole design.**
- **Prompts for the big topics**, so honesty is structured rather than exposed: children, church and tradition, money, where to live, past marriages. Talking through these together is the core of marriage preparation (FOCCUS and PREPARE/ENRICH are used by churches, from knowledge).
- **No chaperone available:** say kindly that many people ask a friend from church. Later, offer vetted volunteer chaperones through partner churches, if the safeguarding adviser agrees. Until then the person can still answer questions and receive introductions but not open a conversation. Owner decision.
- **Meeting in person:** a public place, with the chaperone told beforehand. The app never shares anyone's location.

## Brief Q7: no hooks, and the feelings that matter

**Never build these:** swiping; streaks; badges; likes, views or "people interested" counts; "who liked you" blurred behind a paywall; boosts or paying to be seen; infinite feeds; read receipts, typing dots or "last active"; expiry timers; "X people near you"; percentage compatibility scores; night-time pushes.

**Hope without anxiety**
- Introductions come on a predictable day. Unpredictable rewards drive checking (variable-ratio reinforcement, from knowledge), and checking for compulsive dating-app use has been measured (Orosz et al. 2018 [from knowledge]).
- Notifications only when someone needs to act: an introduction, or a chaperoned message, gathered into one note a day. None between 10pm and 7am, none on Sundays.
- Honest words when the pool is small: "Few people here share all your must-haves right now. That's about who has joined, not about you." **Never suggest loosening a faith must-have.** If the app offers to widen anything, it offers only distance or age, and only when asked.
- Show agreement in words, not scores: "You agree on all your must-haves." "Worth talking about: where to marry, children's schooling." Differences become topics for conversation, not demerits.

**Rejection without shame**
- Double-blind introductions, neutral closing words, no reasons, no timing clues, and no "declined", "rejected" or "passed" anywhere, including emails and notifications.
- After an ending: a short optional pause and one line of comfort (pastoral wording). Nothing ever appears that ranks or compares the person.

**Waiting and singleness**
- Single life is complete and good, not a waiting room (pastoral reviewer: 1 Corinthians 7 and the traditions' teaching on celibacy and vocation).
- "Pause introductions" at any time, for any length, with no penalty and no "you'll lose your place".
- No ages, no "time is running out", nothing about fertility or a "biological clock", no "others your age". Fear of being single goes with settling for less (Spielmann et al. 2013 [verified]). The app must lower that fear, not feed it.

**Engagement without pressure**
- The app never asks "Ready to propose?". Engagement is something the couple tells the app, not a stage the app pushes.
- When they do tell it: a quiet blessing, both accounts close, and data is deleted on a timetable the couple can see. Leaving well is the success.
- Testimonies only if both people opt in, after a gap, on the website or in marketing, never in-app feeds that singles scroll past. Never count marriages in the app.

## Where a nudge could cause guilt, anxiety, compulsion or shame

| Nudge | Feeling it risks | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Intent promise quoted back ("You promised…") | Guilt | Never quote it back |
| "No introductions this week" | Shame | Explain it's about who's here; give no counts |
| Unpredictable arrivals | Compulsive checking | Fixed day; one note a day |
| Percentage match | Anxiety, perfectionism | Words only |
| "They haven't replied" / read receipts | Anxiety, rumination | No receipts; quiet introductions close on their own |
| Snap close after photo reveal | Shame about looks | Simultaneous reveal; a day's pause; endings carry no timing clues |
| "Need a chaperone to continue" | Shame, exclusion | Kind wording; volunteer route later |
| Chaperone sees everything | Self-censorship; control | Big-topic prompts; private help line; change chaperone privately |
| Decision-point reminders | Pressure to rush or to settle | Include "I need more time"; never suggest engagement |
| "Widen your preferences" | Spiritual pressure to compromise | Only distance or age, only when asked |
| Theology questions | Scrupulosity, "failing" the faith test | "Not sure" and "It depends" allowed; never right or wrong; no faith score |
| Marital-history label | Shame for divorced or annulled people | Shown only to people open to it, in the person's chosen neutral words, with the kind explanation the brief asks for |
| Success stories in-app | Comparison | Website only, with consent |
| Pause and come back | Loss aversion | "We'll be here when you're ready." No lost place |
| Subscription renewal while single | Feeling of paying to fail | See Questions for the owner |

## Recommendations

There is no store deadline for this app yet, so: **Must** = in the prototype design. **Should** = first release. **Later** = after launch.

1. **Must, small.** Rename "match" to "introduction" everywhere. *Supports purpose and seriousness.*
2. **Must, small.** Short first-person intent promise at sign-up, never quoted back (pastoral wording). *Supports commitment without guilt.*
3. **Must, medium.** One open conversation at a time, plus at most one introduction waiting for an answer. *Reduces choice overload and the rejection mind-set.*
4. **Must, medium.** Introductions on a fixed weekday, a few at most, no browsing, nothing on Sundays. *Hope without compulsive checking.*
5. **Must, medium.** Double-blind yes; neutral "This introduction has closed"; unanswered introductions close on their own. *Rejection without shame; no limbo.*
6. **Must, medium.** Decision points at each stage with Continue / I need more time / Close kindly, private and joint. Timelines as guides, never countdowns. *Deciding, not sliding, and no rushing.*
7. **Must, small.** Kind endings from checked closing words, no reasons, no timing clues, an optional pause after. *Dignity and closure.*
8. **Must, medium.** Photos: mutual, simultaneous reveal after the conversation starts, before meeting. Up to three, no filters, private verification selfie. *Words before looks.*
9. **Must, small.** Design rule: no attractiveness, popularity or "desirability" scores in matching; no percentage scores on screen. Show agreement and differences in words. *No looks-first ranking, no false precision.*
10. **Must, medium.** Chaperone rules: a table of four, chaperones can't approve, veto or close, a chaperone promise, change chaperone privately, both sides consent. *Being held without being controlled.*
11. **Must, medium.** A private "I feel pressured / I'm worried about my safety" line that no chaperone or match can see, leading to help numbers (999, Forced Marriage Unit, Samaritans) and same-day review. Safeguarding adviser to check. *Safety for the most vulnerable.*
12. **Must, small.** The never-build list in Q7 written into the covenant CLAUDE.md as rules. *Keeps the design honest under commercial pressure.*
13. **Should, small.** "Not sure" and "It depends" answers in Tier 1; questions framed as "what you believe and practise", never right or wrong. *Competence, not exam anxiety.*
14. **Should, medium.** Big-topic conversation prompts (children, church, money, home, the past). *Honest disclosure despite an audience.*
15. **Should, small.** A one-day pause on decisions after photo reveal (owner to decide whether it is firm or advisory). *Slows snap judgements.*
16. **Should, small.** "Pause introductions" at any time, no penalty; honest words when numbers are small; never suggest relaxing faith must-haves. *Waiting without fear or compromise.*
17. **Should, small.** Engagement: the couple tells the app; a quiet blessing; accounts close and data is deleted on a visible timetable; testimonies only with both people's consent, off-app. *Celebration without pressure or comparison.*
18. **Should, small.** Notifications: one daily note only when action is needed; none 10pm–7am or on Sundays. *Calm.*
19. **Later, large.** Vetted volunteer chaperones through partner churches (safeguarding adviser first). *Includes people with no chaperone.*
20. **Later, medium.** Marriage preparation with your church as a suggested step before engagement, linked to the church's own course. *Marrying well, with the church.*

## Questions for the owner

1. **How many at once:** one open conversation, or up to two?
2. **No chaperone:** can someone answer questions and receive introductions while they look for a chaperone, or not join until they have one? Do you want volunteer chaperones from churches later?
3. **Photo timing:** after the conversation has started and both say yes (my advice), or straight after the introduction?
4. **Pause after photo reveal:** firm (decisions open the next day) or advisory only?
5. **Can chaperones be a parent?** Parents bring the most wisdom and the most risk of pressure. Allow them, with the safeguards above, or recommend a friend or pastor first?
6. **Pricing and purpose:** a monthly subscription earns most from people who stay single. Would you consider a pay-once or per-season price (for example, six months), with nothing paid to be seen and nothing that makes a paying person more visible? The product and marketing reviewers should cost it.
7. **Data after engagement:** delete both accounts automatically, or let the couple choose?
8. **Introduction day:** which weekday (not Sunday)?
