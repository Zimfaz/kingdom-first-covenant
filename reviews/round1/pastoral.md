# Kingdom First Covenant: pastoral, theological and safeguarding review (round 1)

Pastoral reviewer, 23 Nov 2026. Answers brief questions 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7, with short notes on the others. Nothing is built; this is advice for the owner.

**Marks.** **V** = verified today (source in brackets, full links in `kfl-pastoral/library/marriage-and-remarriage-traditions.md` and `library/safeguarding/README.md` §5a). **K** = from knowledge; a priest, minister, canonist or the solicitor must check it before it goes on screen. Scripture is BSB, checked word for word against `www/bible/bsb` today.

---

## 0. In brief

The idea is good and pastorally sound. Many churches have no way to help their single adults meet, and many widowed, divorced and annulled Christians feel unseen. Chaperoned introductions fit Catholic, Orthodox and many evangelical and African and Asian diaspora instincts better than any dating app on the market.

Six things matter most:

1. **Ask fairly.** Every answer is written in each tradition's own words. No answer is labelled "biblical" and none is the default. One reader from each tradition checks the bank.
2. **Match quietly, never score.** No "% match". Show "What you share" and "Things to talk about". People are never shown to anyone who has ruled them out, so nobody sees a "no".
3. **Tell the truth about remarriage.** A Catholic can't marry a divorced person (of any church) in church without a declaration of nullity (**V**, CBCEW). The app says so before people choose, kindly, and lets each person choose.
4. **The chaperone protects. It must never control.** Forced marriage and family pressure are real risks. Each person brings their own chaperone and can change them at any time without giving a reason. A chaperone can never end an introduction or run a profile.
5. **Scams and abuse are the biggest dangers on any dating service** (**V**, Ofcom, 18 May 2026). Rules: no money, ever; conversations stay in the app; text only; staff can be reached privately; UK help lines in Help.
6. **Never promise a spouse.** Marriage is a good gift; so is a single life lived for God. Nobody is "waiting to become complete".

## 1. House words for Covenant

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| introduction, introduced | match, matched (inside the app) | The owner's own word is "introduces". "Match" sounds like a dating app and a contest. |
| close this introduction | reject, unmatch, pass, decline | Ending kindly is part of looking for marriage well. |
| What you share · Things to talk about | % match, compatibility score, "great match", "perfect match" | People are not scores (pastoral-tone §16). |
| my yes | pledge, vow, oath, swear | Matthew 5:37; some Anabaptists, Quakers and others won't take oaths (James 5:12). A vow belongs to the wedding. |
| chaperone (the owner's word); "your chaperone" | guardian, wali, minder | A chaperone is a companion who keeps you both safe, not someone in charge of you. |
| free to marry | eligible, available, single (as a status) | "Free to marry" is how churches and the law speak. |
| widowed · divorced · declaration of nullity (annulment) | "divorcee", "remarried", "second-hand", "previously married" as a filter label | Each is named plainly and with dignity. |
| a single life lived for God | "still single", "on the shelf", "waiting for your Boaz", "your season", "the One", "soulmate" | Singleness is a gift (1 Corinthians 7:7), not a waiting room. |
| moving towards marriage | relationship status, "official", "DTR" | It keeps the purpose in view. |

Never on screen: likes, views, "seen", "last active", "popular", "in demand", "boost", "spotlight", "running out of time", any countdown, any number of people who "liked" or "viewed" you.

## 2. Tier 1: the faith and marriage questions (brief Q2)

### 2.1 How it works

- **35 questions in six groups**, about 12–15 minutes. Answers can be changed at any time. Every question has "I'd rather talk about this in person" except the four entry questions. That answer is never treated as a mismatch.
- **Four kinds of question:**
  - **Entry**: answered the same way by everyone who joins (age, free to marry, here to marry, Christian faith).
  - **Core**: always matched. Two people are never introduced if their answers clash (e.g. "I'd like children" and "I don't want children").
  - **My must** (up to 5): the person may tick a question as "This must agree for me". Five is the limit, because small traditions (Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Eastern Catholic) are a small pool in the UK, and too many musts would leave some people with nobody. Under the ticks: "Fewer musts usually means more introductions. The rest can be talked through."
  - **Weighed** or **Shared**: either counted gently or simply shown, never as a dealbreaker.
- **What people see**: "What you share" (three to five plain lines) and "Things to talk about" (differences, put as questions for the chaperoned conversation). Never a number.
- **Fairness**: no tradition is listed as the norm. Options use each church's own words (Theotokos, Holy Communion, Eucharist, the Lord's Supper). Nobody's answer is marked as more faithful. One reader each from the Catholic, Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Anglican and evangelical traditions checks the final wording (as for the church calendar).
- **No ethnicity filter.** Language and culture questions are allowed (2.7). A race filter sits badly with Galatians 3:28 and raises Equality Act questions (solicitor). **Owner decision.**

### 2.2 Group A: Entry (everyone, before anything else)

**Why A4.** Every tradition in the brief needs Trinitarian faith for a church wedding. Thyateira: "The Orthodox Church does not perform marriages between Christians and non-Christians" (**V**). I recommend not, gently, with this exit:

> **This app is for Christians who share this faith**
> Kingdom First Covenant introduces people for a Christian marriage that every main church can bless. If you're still exploring, we're really glad you're asking. A local church, or our sister app Kingdom First Life, could be a good place to start.

## 3. Marital history (brief Q3)

### 3.1 What each church teaches (for the app's explanations)

| Church | Teaching in brief | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Catholic** | A valid marriage lasts for life, so civil divorce alone doesn't free someone to marry in church. A declaration of nullity "signifies that a true bond of marriage never existed". Children are legitimate. "No Catholic priest can conduct the marriage of a divorced person unless or until the first marriage has been declared null", whether or not that person is Catholic. Don't set a wedding date while a case is pending. No case is refused for inability to pay. About a year, 6–18 months. Widowed people are free to marry. | **V** CBCEW/NBCW guide; CCC 1629 |
| **Eastern Orthodox** | Marriage is one and for life, and the Church "stands opposed to divorce". By economia ("out of concern for the spiritual well being of the parties") a second "and even a third" marriage may be allowed, with a service that "bears a penitential character". Never a fourth (**K**). A church divorce from the bishop normally follows the civil one (**K**). | **V** OCA |
| **Oriental Orthodox** | Each church has its own rules, often stricter. Coptic Orthodox: remarriage after divorce generally only after adultery (**K**). | **K**: a reader from each church |
| **Church of England** | Since 2002: "there are exceptional circumstances in which a divorced person may be married in church during the lifetime of a former spouse". The decision "rests with the minister", who may decline (Matrimonial Causes Act 1965 s8(2)). A Service of Prayer and Dedication after a civil marriage is another way. | **V** Diocese of London 2023; legislation.gov.uk |
| **Protestant** | Varies. Many allow remarriage after adultery or desertion (Matthew 19:9; 1 Corinthians 7:15), some more widely, a few not while a former spouse lives. The Methodist Church allows it at the minister's discretion (**K**). | **K** |
| **All** | A widowed person is free to marry: "A wife is bound to her husband as long as he lives. But if her husband dies, she is free to marry anyone she wishes, as long as he belongs to the Lord." (1 Corinthians 7:39) | BSB |

### 3.2 The marital history screen (exact wording)

> **Your story so far**
> Every story is welcome here. Choose the one that's true for you. You won't need to explain it on your profile, and nobody will ever see who has chosen not to be introduced to them.
> - I've never been married
> - I'm widowed
> - I'm divorced
> - I'm divorced, and my marriage has been declared null by the Church (declaration of nullity, often called an annulment)
> - I'm divorced, and my church has given permission to marry again (for example an Orthodox church divorce)
> - I'm divorced, and a declaration of nullity or church permission is in process
>
> *Why we ask:* churches have different teaching about marriage after divorce. Telling the truth here means you're only introduced to people who can marry you with a clear conscience, in a church that can bless it.

Under **widowed**, one quiet line: "We're sorry for your loss. Grief has no timetable, and there's no right moment to begin again. Go at your own pace."

Under **divorced**: "You don't have to explain what happened. If your marriage ended because you were being hurt, you did not fail God by getting safe." Then a small "Help and support ›" link (domestic abuse lines, 6.6).

**Never show Malachi 2:16** ("For I hate divorce…") or any "God hates divorce" line anywhere in the app. Its own BSB wording goes on, "He who divorces his wife covers his garment with violence", and it is too often used against people who left a violent marriage.

### 3.3 "Who are you open to?" (exact wording)

> **Who would you be open to marrying?**
> Tick everyone you could marry with a clear conscience. You can change this at any time, and nobody is told what you chose.
> ☐ Never married ☐ Widowed ☐ Divorced, with a declaration of nullity ☐ Divorced, with church permission to marry again ☐ Divorced ☐ Declaration of nullity or permission in process
> [Read what my church teaches ›]

**Nothing is ticked in advance**, whatever someone's church. "Read what my church teaches" opens the paragraph for their own church only (3.4), and the others are one tap away. The app explains. It never ticks or unticks a box for anyone. A Catholic may know their pastor's advice, or be open to a divorced Christian who would ask for a declaration of nullity.

Matching is **two-way and silent**: A is shown to B only if each fits the other's choices. Nobody ever sees "not open to divorced people".

### 3.4 Each church's explanation (exact wording, for the reader from each tradition to check)

**Catholic**
> The Catholic Church teaches that a valid marriage lasts for life, so a civil divorce on its own doesn't free someone to marry again in church. A declaration of nullity (often called an annulment) is the Church's finding, after careful inquiry, that a true marriage bond never existed. It isn't a "Catholic divorce", and it doesn't make children of that marriage illegitimate. This applies to anyone a Catholic marries, whether or not they're Catholic. A widowed person is free to marry. If you're unsure, your parish priest can help. Nobody is turned away from the process because they can't pay.

**Orthodox (Eastern)**
> The Orthodox Church holds that marriage is for life, and it grieves when a marriage breaks down. Out of care for its people (this is called economia), a bishop may give a church divorce and allow a second, or even a third, marriage, usually with a quieter service of repentance and hope. Each diocese has its own process, so your priest will guide you.

**Oriental Orthodox** (Coptic, Ethiopian, Eritrean, Armenian, Syriac, Malankara)
> Your church has its own teaching on marriage after divorce, and some of it is stricter than in other churches. Please ask your priest before you choose.

**Anglican**
> The Church of England teaches that marriage is for life. Since 2002 it has accepted that, in exceptional circumstances, a divorced person may marry in church while a former husband or wife is still living. The decision rests with the priest, who may also say no. A service of prayer and dedication after a civil wedding is another way. The Church in Wales, the Scottish Episcopal Church and other Anglican churches have their own guidance.

**Protestant and free churches**
> Churches differ. Many allow remarriage after divorce, some only where there was adultery or desertion (Matthew 19:9; 1 Corinthians 7:15), and a few not while a former husband or wife is living. Your church's leaders can tell you how they see it.

Foot line on every version: "Your church's teaching and your priest's or pastor's guidance come first. This is only a short summary."

### 3.5 Hard cases

- **Separated, not yet divorced.** Not eligible. The law and every church see the person as married (bigamy is a crime). Exit screen:
  > **Kingdom First Covenant is for people who are free to marry now**
  > If you're separated, please come back when your divorce is final. We know waiting can be hard. If you left because you weren't safe, you did the right thing. Help is here whenever you need it ›
- **Declaration of nullity or church permission in process.** **Owner decision.** My advice (to check with a Catholic canonist): the person can make a profile and is introduced only to people who ticked "in process". Their profile says so plainly. The "Moving towards marriage" step stays closed until they say the decision has been given. The step's line: "When your church has given its decision, you can tell us here." This follows the CBCEW guide ("unwise… to set a wedding date before an annulment has been granted"), which does not forbid the person getting to know someone. Some priests will advise waiting altogether, and the help line says so: "Some priests advise waiting until the decision is given. Please ask yours."
- **Proof.** Ask for **no documents** about marriage, divorce or nullity. They are special category data under UK GDPR (religion, sex life), and holding them is a target for misuse. Instead: (a) a clear declaration in "my yes" that the answers are true, with the warning that false statements end membership; (b) the reminder that "every church will ask for proof that you're free to marry before your wedding" (**V** for the Church of England: clergy check the decree absolute, now the final order; Catholic and Orthodox prenuptial enquiries **K**); (c) the chaperone stage includes a "Before you say yes" conversation guide (5.6) with "Your past marriage, if there was one, and any children" on it. Optional later: a **church reference** (a minister confirms the person belongs to their church, never their marital details).
- **Clergy.** Catholic and Orthodox clergy have their own rules (an Orthodox priest who is widowed does not remarry, **K**). One line in Help: "If you're ordained, please speak to your bishop first."

## 4. Tier 2: pictures (brief Q4, short pastoral note)

- Photos only after both have said "Yes, I'd like to keep talking" at Tier 1, and **both chaperones are present**. Then reveal both at once, never one-sided.
- Modesty in words everyone can share: "Clear, recent photos of your face, as you'd dress for church or a family meal." Moderated before anyone sees them. No filters or face editing. No swimwear or gym-mirror photos.
- No photo of children (other people's privacy and safeguarding), and nothing showing your home, street, car number plate or workplace.
- Strip location data from every photo.

## 5. Chaperones (brief Q5)

### 5.1 Pastoral wisdom

A chaperone is old Christian wisdom and many cultures' practice. It protects the vulnerable, slows things down, adds a second pair of eyes, and brings the community in early (Ecclesiastes 4:12: "…a cord of three strands is not quickly broken."). But a chaperone can also be the person applying pressure: a parent pushing a match, a church leader with too much power, or a "friend" who is part of a scam. So the design keeps the chaperone **as a witness and a support, never a decision-maker**.

### 5.2 Who can be a chaperone

- An adult (18+) who **knows the person in real life**: a parent, sibling, other relative, godparent, friend, priest, pastor, minister, home-group leader or mentor.
- Each person brings **their own** chaperone. Every conversation has at least four people: two members and two chaperones. A second chaperone each (e.g. a parent and a priest) is allowed.
- **Not**: a current or former partner; someone who is also a member looking for marriage; anyone under 18; the other person's chaperone; anyone the member has met only online.
- Each chaperone passes the same **age and identity check** as members, accepts the **chaperone promise** (5.5), and may chaperone **no more than three people** at a time. That limit stops scam rings and "professional" chaperones.
- Roles are self-described ("my mother", "my parish priest"). The app never shows "Rev.", "Fr" or "Pastor" as a verified title unless it has been checked through a partner church, because people pretend to be clergy (KFL safeguarding rules 8.6).

### 5.3 What a chaperone sees and can do

| Sees | Doesn't see |
|---|---|
| The whole introduction conversation, from the start | The member's other introductions, past or present |
| The other person's first name, church family and "What you share / Things to talk about" | Marital history detail beyond the label; "Who are you open to?"; My musts |
| Photos, once both members have revealed them | Location, payment, private notes, safety reports, anything the member sent to staff |

| Can | Can't |
|---|---|
| Write in the conversation | Message anyone privately: not the other member, the other chaperone, or anyone else (owner's rule: no private messages) |
| Ask for a pause: "I'd like us to pause and talk this through" (the conversation waits until the member taps Continue) | Close the introduction, accept it, or move it forward |
| Report a worry to the safety team, privately | Create, edit or run a profile, or answer questions for the member |
| Step down at any time | Read anything after stepping down or being removed |

### 5.4 The member stays free

- The member can **change or remove their chaperone at any time, without giving a reason**. The conversation pauses until a new chaperone joins. The old chaperone sees only: "You're no longer chaperoning this conversation. Thank you for your care."
- Sign-up, the four entry questions, "my yes" and every decision to move forward happen **on the member's own phone, signed in as them**. Profiles made by parents are not allowed ("If someone else made this profile for you, please tell us ›").
- A **"Talk to us privately"** button on every screen reaches the safety team, not another user. It is the one private channel, and it fits the no-private-messages rule because it is staff, not members.

### 5.5 The chaperone promise (exact wording)

> **Thank you for being [first name]'s chaperone**
> You're here as a friend and a witness, to help [first name] and the person they're getting to know stay safe, honest and kind. The decisions are theirs.
> - I'll keep what I read here private. I won't screenshot or share it.
> - I won't message anyone privately, inside or outside the app.
> - I'll speak up gently if something worries me, and report anything unsafe.
> - I won't push either of them towards marriage or away from it.
> - I'm 18 or over, and I'm not looking for marriage here myself.
>
> "…clothe yourselves with hearts of compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience." Colossians 3:12
>
> [I promise] [Not now]

### 5.6 What happens when no one is available

Many people have no family to ask: converts, people estranged from family, widowed and older people, people new to the UK, people who left an abusive home.

> **Who could be your chaperone?**
> It could be a friend, a relative, someone from your church, a home-group leader or a mentor. They don't need to live near you; they'll join the conversation on their own phone.
> Not having someone to ask is nothing to be ashamed of. You can still answer your questions and get your profile ready. Introductions begin once your chaperone has said yes.
> [Invite a chaperone] [I'll do this later]

Later (not at launch): **volunteer chaperones from partner churches**, with safer recruitment, training, references, DBS where eligible, supervision and a named safeguarding lead (**adviser**). Never a paid or anonymous "Covenant chaperone" before then.

### 5.7 Meeting in person (exact checklist)

> **Before you meet**
> - Have a video call first, with your chaperones, so you both know the other person is real.
> - Meet somewhere public in daylight: a café, or coffee after a church service. Your chaperone or a family member comes too, or stays close by.
> - Get there and home on your own. Don't share your home address or workplace yet.
> - Tell your chaperone where you'll be and when you'll be home.
> - No one should ever ask you for money. If they do, tell us.
> - If anything feels wrong, leave. You don't need a reason.

The app shares **no location** between users, ever (KFL rule 2.2). Meeting plans are typed in the chaperoned conversation, not tracked.

### 5.8 Towards engagement, or a kind close

Steps, shown in words only (no progress bar, no ticks, no timers): **Introduced → Talking, with your chaperones → Photos shared → Meeting in person → Meeting families and church → Moving towards marriage.**

- **"Before you say yes"** conversation guide (offered, never required): faith and church; where you'd marry and worship; children; money and debts; health you'd want a spouse to know about; your past marriage, if there was one, and any children; family expectations; how you handle conflict. "Some of this is best talked through with your priest or pastor."
- **Moving towards marriage** needs both to say yes. Line: "We're so glad. May the Lord bless you as you prepare for marriage. Your next step is to speak to your priest or pastor about marriage preparation."
- **Closing an introduction**: either person, at any time, no reason needed (5.9 and 7.3).

### 5.9 Closing (exact wording)

For the person closing:
> **Close this introduction?**
> It's right to close an introduction when you don't believe you're being led towards marriage together. You don't have to give a reason. We'll let [first name] and both chaperones know kindly.
> Add a short message if you'd like (optional):
> ○ Thank you for the time we've shared. I don't believe we're being led towards marriage, and I wish you every blessing.
> ○ Thank you for your honesty and kindness. I'm praying God's best for you.
> [Close kindly] [Not now]
>
> "Because Joseph her husband was a righteous man and was unwilling to disgrace her publicly, he resolved to divorce her quietly." Matthew 1:19 *(shown only to the person closing, as an example of ending things without shame; never to the person receiving it)*

Only these template messages, no free text: someone who has just been turned down shouldn't receive hurtful words. Two people who close an introduction are never introduced again unless both later ask.

## 6. Safeguarding (brief Q6)

### 6.1 The main risks, and the rule for each

| Risk | Rules |
|---|---|
| **Romance fraud** (Ofcom: 29% of adult internet users have met a romance or dating scam, **V**) | No money, gifts, gift cards, crypto or "investment" talk, ever (in the terms). Everything stays in the app ("Anyone who asks to move to WhatsApp or another app before you've met in person is a warning sign"). Video call before meeting. A gentle automatic note when money words appear (it informs and notifies moderators; it decides nothing): "A reminder: never send money, gift cards or crypto to someone you've met here, whatever the reason. If you've been asked, please tell us ›". Scam reports reviewed the same day. |
| **Coercion and forced marriage** (illegal; up to 7 years; includes arranging marriage for someone who lacks mental capacity, **V** gov.uk) | 5.4: the member's own phone and choices; change chaperone silently; "Talk to us privately"; no profiles made by others; Help names the Forced Marriage Unit and Karma Nirvana. Moderators trained to spot family or community pressure and "honour" language. Possible lack of mental capacity is escalated to the safeguarding lead (Mental Capacity Act 2005; **adviser**). |
| **Domestic abuse and coercive control** | "Signs to take seriously" in Help (6.4). D3 frame. Never suggest reconciliation or counselling in abuse (pastoral-tone 7.2). |
| **Spiritual pressure** | Rule and moderation: nobody may claim God has told them someone must marry them. (6.4) |
| **Harassment, stalking, ban evasion** (Ofcom, **V**) | Block, report and "Hide me from someone" (a former spouse's or abuser's phone or email, so they never see the profile). Town or county only, never exact location. Notifications never name anyone ("You have a message in Kingdom First Covenant"). "Leave quickly" button. Re-registration checks on banned identities. |
| **Intimate images, sextortion** (Ofcom, **V**) | Conversations are text only. No images, voice or files (photos only through moderated Tier 2). Sharing intimate images without consent is a crime (**K**); the Revenge Porn Helpline is in Help. |
| **Under-18s** | Age and identity check for members and chaperones. Ofcom expects a children's access assessment (**V**); KFL rule 1.2 applies. |
| **Older, recently bereaved and isolated people** (targets for fraud) | Nothing restricts them. The chaperone, the no-money rule and the scam note protect them. Hourglass in Help. |
| **Abuse of power by clergy or church leaders as chaperones** | Concerns go to the safety team, then to that church's safeguarding team or Thirtyone:eight, never only to "the leadership" (pastoral-tone 8.3). |

### 6.2 People leaving abusive marriages

- If still married, they can't join yet (3.5), but they must never feel pushed out. The exit screen says "you did the right thing" and links to help.
- After divorce: no need to explain; "Hide me from someone"; no photos until they choose; discreet notifications; "Leave quickly".
- **Never** show Malachi 2:16, "God hates divorce", or lines about "standing for your marriage" anywhere.
- Moderators never contact a former spouse and never mediate.

### 6.3 Law and process (for the solicitor and the independent safeguarding adviser)

- **Online Safety Act**: user-to-user service with messaging. Illegal-content risk assessment (Ofcom lists 17 kinds), clear terms, complaints, fast removal, children's access assessment, records (**V**, Ofcom dating guidance 18 May 2026). NCA reporting of CSEA (KFL rules 3.9).
- **UK GDPR special category data**: religion, sex life (marital history, D2, E2), and biometric data if face checks are used to identify people. Needs explicit consent, a DPIA and data minimisation, hence no documents (3.5). When a couple both say they're engaged, or someone leaves: delete the profile, answers and conversations within an agreed time, keeping only safety records for the agreed retention period (**adviser + solicitor**).
- **Equality Act 2010**: the religious-organisation exception in Sch. 23 para 2 "does not apply to an organisation whose sole or main purpose is commercial" (**V**). So Christians-only membership, man–woman-only introductions and any ethnicity filter all need the solicitor's advice **before** anything is published. Theologically, every church in this app teaches marriage as between one man and one woman (D1). That is the app's purpose, said plainly and without contempt for anyone.
- **Criminal records**: whether the terms can exclude people with convictions for sexual or violent offences (Rehabilitation of Offenders Act; **solicitor**). Point users to the Domestic Violence Disclosure Scheme ("Clare's Law"; **K**) in Help.
- **People**: a named safeguarding lead and deputy, safer recruitment for moderators, two-step sign-in for every admin account, written moderation guide (KFL rules 5 and 7 carry over).

## 7. Hope, waiting, singleness and rejection (brief Q7)

### 7.1 The theology in five lines

1. **Marriage is a good gift.** "…It is not good for the man to be alone. I will make for him a suitable helper." (Genesis 2:18).
2. **So is a single life lived for God.** Jesus was single. Paul calls both gifts: "…each man has his own gift from God; one has this gift, another has that." (1 Corinthians 7:7). Catholic and Orthodox Christians honour consecrated and monastic life as a calling, never a consolation prize.
3. **God promises Himself, not a spouse.** Never lift Jeremiah 29:11, Psalm 37:4 or Psalm 68:6 ("God settles the lonely in families…") into a promise of marriage. Waiting in Scripture is waiting *for the LORD*: "The LORD is good to those who wait for Him, to the soul who seeks Him." (Lamentations 3:25).
4. **Worth is given, not won.** No closed introduction, age, past marriage or empty week says anything about someone's worth to God.
5. **Hope without anxiety.** No pressure about time ("biological clock", "running out of time", age nudges), no comparison, no pressure to make anyone feel chosen.

### 7.2 Behaviour rules (from the pastoral side; full list for the behavioural expert)

No swiping, likes, views, "seen" or "last active", streaks, badges, boosts, counts of interest, or infinite browsing. Introductions arrive slowly, a few at a time. No notifications about other people's engagements. Nothing rewards time in the app. Quiet hours: no notifications after 10pm, and none on Sunday mornings unless the person chooses. One introduction moving towards marriage at a time ("let your yes be yes"; Matthew 5:37: "Simply let your ‘Yes’ be ‘Yes,’ and your ‘No,’ ‘No.’…").

### 7.3 Key screens (exact wording)

**Welcome (first screen)**
> **Kingdom First Covenant**
> Introductions for marriage, not dating.
> We introduce Christians who hope to marry, slowly and with care, in conversations that always include a chaperone you trust.
> Marriage is a good gift from God. So is a single life lived for Him. You're not waiting to become complete.
> "…each man has his own gift from God; one has this gift, another has that." 1 Corinthians 7:7

**A prayer before you begin** (optional, offered once and in Help; written for every tradition):
> Lord, You know me better than I know myself. Give me honesty, kindness and courage. Lead me in Your way, towards marriage or not, and keep my heart at rest in You. Amen.

Catholic and Orthodox users may also see, labelled for their tradition only, the prayer of Tobias and Sarah (Tobit 8:5–8), a traditional wedding reading in both churches. The BSB has no Tobit, so quote it from a cleared Bible (e.g. eng-webbe) once that is in place (**owner decision**; copyright check).

**No new introductions this week**
> No new introductions this week. That isn't a verdict on you; we introduce people slowly, on purpose.
> "The LORD is good to those who wait for Him, to the soul who seeks Him." Lamentations 3:25

**When someone closes an introduction (the person receiving it)**
> **[First name] has closed this introduction**
> [Their chosen message, if any.]
> It's all right to feel sad. An introduction closing kindly is part of looking for marriage well. It says nothing about your worth to God.
> "…Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; you are Mine!" Isaiah 43:1
> [Finding this hard? Get help ›]

The chaperones see only: "This introduction has closed. Thank you for your care."

**Leaving without a marriage**
> **Thank you for the care you've shown here**
> Leaving without a marriage isn't failing. Your life with God isn't on hold, and nothing can separate you from His love.
> "…nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord." Romans 8:39
> Your profile, answers and conversations will be deleted [within N days]. *(N for the solicitor)*

**Engaged (both say yes)**
> **We're so glad**
> May the Lord bless you both as you prepare for marriage. Your next step is to speak to your priest or pastor about marriage preparation.
> "…a cord of three strands is not quickly broken." Ecclesiastes 4:12
> Your profiles are now closed to new introductions. Nothing is announced to anyone else.

Engagements are **never announced** to other users. Testimonies only with both people's written consent, after the wedding, moderated, and never pushed to people who are waiting. Marketing never counts couples ("500 engagements!"), because a count tells the people still waiting that they are behind.

## 8. Short notes on the other questions

- **Q1 Purpose.** "My yes" (A3) instead of a pledge; one introduction moving towards marriage at a time; a gentle check-in (not a countdown) after about three months: "Would you like to talk with your chaperones about where this is going?"
- **Q8 Product.** Must-haves from the pastoral side: B1–B2, marital history and openness, chaperones, closing kindly, Help, private safety channel, block, report, "Hide me from someone". Later: volunteer chaperones, church references, Tobit prayer.
- **Q9 Market.** Approach churches with the same humility as KFL: "with your church", never "instead of"; the priest or pastor prepares the couple.
- **Q10 Brand.** "Covenant" is a good word for marriage across all the traditions (Malachi 2:14 calls a wife "your companion and your wife by covenant", BSB checked; the verse is about broken faith, so it suits teaching, not a banner). Keep "Kingdom First" and never pair it with promised outcomes (KFL brand rule).

## 9. Decisions for the owner

1. **Who can join (A4):** Trinitarian Christians only, with the kind exit screen? (Recommended. Solicitor to check against the Equality Act, 6.3.)
2. **Man–woman marriage only (D1):** as all the churches teach. Solicitor first, because the religious-organisation exception doesn't cover commercial companies (**V**).
3. **Declaration of nullity in process (3.5):** introduced only to people who are open to it, with "Moving towards marriage" closed until the decision is given (recommended), or no introductions until it's granted?
4. **No documents for marital status (3.5):** self-declaration plus the church's own checks before the wedding (recommended), or some proof?
5. **Two chaperones per conversation, one each (5.2)**, with the member free to change theirs at any time (recommended)?
6. **No ethnicity filter (2.1)** (recommended; solicitor).
7. **"Introduction" instead of "match" inside the app (1)?**
8. **Up to five "My musts" (2.1)?**
9. **Template-only closing messages (5.9)?**
10. **Readers:** one reader each from the Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox (ideally Coptic), Anglican and evangelical traditions to check sections 2 and 3, plus a Catholic canonist for 3.4–3.5, before any wording goes into the prototype.
