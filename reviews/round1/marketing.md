### The model to learn from: Muzz (Muslim marriage app)
- UK company (MUZZ LTD), "20 million Muslims looking for marriage", "more than 1,000,000 Muslim marriages have already started", 40,000 ratings, 4.4 stars [V](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id969997496).
- "Include a chaperone (or Wali) in your conversations" [V](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id969997496). In practice it is **optional**: the wali receives **weekly chat transcripts**; photos can be **blurred** and shown to chosen matches only; **selfie verification**; an oath on joining; feedback on seriousness affects how often you are shown [V](https://muzz.com/help/getting-started/how-is-muzz-halal).
- Founder's (unverified) claim: chaperoned accounts get better conversations and more marriages; walis can put some people off [V, 2018](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/the-male-chaperones-of-muslim-dating-apps).
- Prices: Gold £14.99 to £34.99, plus paid compliments and boosts at £4.99 [V](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id969997496).
- **Lesson:** marriage intent, family involvement and photo privacy *can* grow a huge UK app. But Muzz made the chaperone a choice. We plan to make it compulsory, which nobody I found does. That is our biggest difference and our biggest risk.

Other faiths (from knowledge, not checked): South Asian matrimony sites such as Shaadi.com let a parent or relative manage a profile [K]; Jewish shidduch matchmaking uses a third-party matchmaker and family checks [K]. Both show that "a trusted third person in the room" is normal in many UK communities, including many of the Orthodox and African and Asian diaspora churches we want to reach [K].

### Gap we can fill
Nobody in the UK combines: **marriage only + faith questions first + photos second + a chaperone in every conversation + every Christian tradition with honest filters + divorced and annulled treated kindly.** Each piece exists somewhere; the combination does not (my search, Nov 2026).

## 2. Audience size and segments

### What the numbers say (with caution)
- 27.5 million people (46.2%) in England and Wales called themselves Christian in Census 2021 [V](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/culturalidentity/religion/bulletins/religionenglandandwales/census2021).
- British Social Attitudes 2025: **5% of all adults attend church weekly**; Catholics attend most often (15% weekly among Catholics); no sign of a youth revival [V](https://natcen.ac.uk/no-revival-sight-church-attendance-britain-remains-below-pre-pandemic-levels).
- **Warning:** the Bible Society's "Quiet Revival" figures (monthly attendance 8% to 12%, 18-24s 4% to 16%, young men 21%) [V](https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2025/11-april/news/uk/dramatic-growth-in-young-people-attending-church-bible-society-research-finds) were reported by NatCen as "undermined by apparently fraudulent responses", with YouGov to repeat the survey [V](https://natcen.ac.uk/no-revival-sight-church-attendance-britain-remains-below-pre-pandemic-levels). **Do not quote them in public material.** The one useful pointer it gives: Catholics are a large and growing share of young churchgoers (41% of 18-34 churchgoers in that survey) [V, same caveat].
- Weddings in England and Wales, 2023: 216,901 opposite-sex; only 32,473 (about 15%) religious; Church of England and Church in Wales 23,004; Roman Catholic 3,303 [V](https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2025/21-november/news/uk/church-of-england-weddings-at-historic-low-2023-figures-show). The church-wedding market is small and shrinking, which is part of why single churchgoers struggle to meet.
- **Gender imbalance:** among single practising Christians, about two middle-class single women to every single man (YouGov analysis, about 2015); Catholic churches roughly equal [V](https://singlefriendlychurch.com/news-and-resources/where-are-all-the-men). Church Times (2018) gives about 60:40 [V](https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2018/31-august/news/uk/christian-lonelyhearts-swipe-right).
- **Orthodox:** about 257 Orthodox churches in the UK (Christian Research estimate, about 2018) [V](https://www.christian-research.org/reports/archives-and-statistics/other-uk-churches/index.html). The Romanian community is one of the largest outside Romania [V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Orthodox_Archdiocese_of_Great_Britain_and_Northern_Ireland). I found no reliable count of Orthodox faithful; the census does not split it out in the headline bulletin [V](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/culturalidentity/religion/bulletins/religionenglandandwales/census2021).

### Honest sizing
I will not put a single number on the market: the inputs are too shaky. The practical test is the **waitlist**: if a free waitlist with no paid ads cannot reach about 1,000 serious sign-ups in London within three months, the market is not there yet in that form (my proposed test). Track the sex balance on the waitlist from day one; if women outnumber men 2:1, matching will disappoint many women and marketing must reach men on purpose (Catholic and Orthodox parishes, men's groups, Christian Unions' alumni).

### Segments (proposed)
1. **Practising Catholics, 25-40.** Want a Catholic spouse, understand annulment, used to detailed questions (CatholicMatch). Better sex balance. Largest single opportunity.
2. **Orthodox diaspora, 25-40** (Romanian, Greek and Cypriot, Russian, Ukrainian, Serbian, Bulgarian, Georgian; Oriental Orthodox: Eritrean, Ethiopian, Coptic). Family involvement is normal, so the chaperone fits. Parents may join first. [K]
3. **Evangelical and charismatic, 25-40,** including Black-majority and Pentecostal churches. Most likely to already use SALT or Hinge; need the strongest "why switch".
4. **Anglican and mainline,** often older or rural; smaller pools.
5. **Widowed, divorced and annulled, 35-60.** Badly served, often ashamed in church, older-skewing Christian Connection is their current option. The kind wording on marital history is a real draw here. Some may find a chaperone patronising; give them a pastor or adult friend option and say why.
6. **Parents and godparents** (not users, but buyers of the idea). In diaspora communities they will recommend it. They can be chaperones.

## 3. Product (marketing view)

What makes the promise real, so marketing can say it truthfully:
- **Marriage, not dating** in the store title or subtitle, sign-up, every profile and every match. Only say "marriage" outcomes in words like "introductions for marriage", never "find your spouse" or "you will marry" (no promised outcomes, as in KFL).
- **No swiping, no likes counters, no streaks, no boosts** (behavioural reviewer). This is also the honest pricing story.
- **Faith first, faces second** (Tier 1 then photos). Simple to explain and memorable.
- **Never alone in the conversation** (chaperone). The safety and trust story.
- **Every tradition, your choice** (cross-tradition with "open to" filters).
- **Your story, told kindly** (never married, widowed, divorced, annulled, annulment in process).
- **Leaves when you marry:** offer to close the account and delete data when people marry. A gentle, optional "We're engaged" message only if they want to share it, never a count on the website.

Only claim what is built. Until the prototype exists, the website says "Planned" (KFL's rule).

## 5. Place

- **App Store and Google Play, UK only.** Apple treats dating as a saturated category: new dating apps are not accepted "unless they offer a meaningfully different or improved experience" (guideline 4.3(b)) [V](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Our review notes must explain the compulsory chaperone, theology-first matching and no swiping. User-generated content rules also apply: filtering, reporting, blocking and published contact details (1.2) [V](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Category, age rating and wording to be checked by the product reviewer.
- **Website:** its own domain (check availability) with a waitlist, "how it works", "for churches and parents", safety page, prices on launch day.
- **Churches, parishes and communities:** see Promotion. The ask to clergy is "tell your single adults it exists", never "run it".
- **Later:** Ireland next (English, similar churches) [K]; Romanian or Greek language only with native-speaker and pastoral checks, as for KFL.

## 6. Promotion

### Positioning
**"Introductions for marriage, for Christians of every tradition. Faith first, faces second, and never alone in the conversation."**
Short line for store and social: **"Marriage, not dating."**
Avoid: "find the one God has for you", "your future spouse is waiting", any promised outcome; "church in your pocket"; slighting other apps by name.

### How the two big choices help or hurt marketing

| | Helps | Hurts |
|---|---|---|
| **Compulsory chaperone** | Clear difference; Apple 4.3(b) case; trust for parents, priests and diaspora families; strong romance-scam defence (Ofcom: 29% of adult internet users have met romance or dating scams; 6% on a dating site or app [V](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/dating-and-social-discovery-know-the-online-safety-risks-rules-and-how-to-comply)); good press story | Friction: people without a willing chaperone cannot talk; some adults (35+, widowed, professionals) may feel treated as children; slower conversations; fewer sign-ups than optional models like Muzz |
| **Cross-tradition** | Bigger pools in each area; Catholic men help the evangelical sex imbalance; "every tradition welcome" is unusual and warm | Many Catholics and Orthodox want only their own church (and their churches have rules for mixed marriages [K]); may look like it blurs real differences; any wording mistake offends someone |

How to handle the hurts in marketing: say "you choose who you're open to" in the first line about traditions; show the chaperone as **your** choice of person ("a parent, a friend, your pastor or priest"); publish the theology and marriage questions so each tradition can see it is treated fairly (pastoral reviewer and one reader per tradition check first).

### Channels (cheapest first)
1. **Waitlist site + founder story** (why a marriage app, why a chaperone). Cost: domain and hosting only.
2. **Clergy and parish letters,** one version per tradition: Catholic diocesan marriage and family life offices; Orthodox parish priests by jurisdiction (Greek Thyateira, Romanian, Russian, Serbian, Antiochian, and the Coptic, Eritrean and Ethiopian churches) [K]; Anglican and free-church networks; Black-majority church networks [K]. Ask for a mention, not an endorsement. Owner's time only.
3. **Singles ministries** such as Single Friendly Church [V](https://singlefriendlychurch.com/news-and-resources/where-are-all-the-men), and young-adult groups in parishes. Offer a free talk or guide on "meeting well, with your family" (pastoral reviewer writes it).
4. **Christian media PR:** Premier Christianity and Premier Christian Radio, Church Times, Christian Today, Catholic Herald, The Tablet, Catholic Universe; diaspora press and community Facebook groups (organic). The chaperone is the press hook. Free (time), or a PR freelancer later.
5. **Events:** Christian festivals and Catholic young-adult events [K]; a leaflet table only if the safeguarding adviser is happy. Get stand prices before deciding.
6. **Paid, small:** Apple Search Ads on "christian dating", "christian marriage", "catholic dating", "orthodox dating" (test £300 in launch month, my proposal). **Meta:** dating ads need Meta's prior written permission [K, check the policy page](https://transparency.meta.com/en-gb/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/dating-ads/), and KFL's Instagram was disabled, so no Meta ads without the owner's decision. As with KFL, no targeting by religion (special category data) [K].
7. **Video:** short, silent, calm explainers ("How Covenant works in 30 seconds"), never romance-bait; no stock-photo couples; no real members' faces without written consent.

### Testimonies
Only real, consented, after marriage or engagement, and never counted ("120 couples!") until we can prove the number. No wedding-countdown or "success rate" claims.

## 10. Cross-promotion with KFL, and the risk to KFL's reputation

**Helpful, low-risk links**
- "From the makers of Kingdom First Life" on the Covenant site and store page; a quiet footer link on kingdomfirstlife.com.
- Shared family look and the Christian media contacts built for KFL.
- Later, for married or engaged couples, Covenant can point to KFL for praying and reading together (a natural "next step", not an advert).

**Do not**
- Put Covenant on KFL's Today, Pray, Renew, Live or One Body screens, or send push notices about it. It breaks KFL's "no pressure" rules and feels like selling to people at prayer.
- Use any KFL data (sex, God's Daughter, Care, pregnancy, church choice, prayers) to find or target singles. Ever.
- Email KFL users about Covenant without fresh, separate consent (the solicitor should confirm; the "similar products" exception may not cover a different service [K]).
- Ask KFL founding churches to promote Covenant as part of the KFL relationship.

**Risks to KFL**
- One romance scam, abuse case or safeguarding failure on Covenant becomes "Kingdom First app" in the news, and churches that recommend KFL may step back.
- Opposite-sex-only matching and the divorce and annulment wording could draw criticism that spills onto KFL.
- Owner time pulled from KFL in its first months.
- The shared "Kingdom First" name means both brands rise and fall together.

**Mitigations:** separate company inbox, support, moderation and privacy policy; launch only when section 9 is true; keep Covenant's public tone calm and humble; agree a short crisis statement with the solicitor before beta.
