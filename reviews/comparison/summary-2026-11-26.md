# Covenant compared with other apps (26 Nov 2026)

Marketing reviewer. Full sheet: covenant-app-comparison-2026-11-26.xlsx (22 services plus Covenant; 59 sources, all checked 26 Nov 2026). Rebuild it with build_comparison.py.

Limits:
- Google Play pages would not load. The Play ratings shown come from third-party trackers (AppGoblin and Grand-Screen); the rest say "not checked".
- For eight apps only a non-UK store page could be reached, so their prices are in US$ or € and not converted.

## Decisions for the owner
1. **Video calls.** Should Covenant offer calls with the Companions present? Muzz, Shaadi.com and Bumble have in-app calls. Decide before the beta, with the safeguarding advisers.
2. **A joining pledge.** Muzz asks every new member to accept an oath on conduct. Recommended: a short pledge, worded by the pastoral reviewer.
3. **Church-hosted events while the waitlist is open.** Recommended: yes, tested in London first.

## Top 8 findings
1. **No app makes a chaperone compulsory.** Muzz comes nearest: its optional "Chaperone Mode" sends a wali a weekly copy of the chats. No app checks a chaperone's ID. Covenant's Companion in every conversation is unique, and it is also the biggest source of friction.
2. **"For marriage" no longer sets Covenant apart.** CatholicMatch, Muzz, Shaadi.com and ArkLove all say it plainly. So does Upward since Match Group relaunched it in September 2026 as "modern courtship", and it is no longer Christian-only.
3. **Faith questions are common, but every app shows photos from the start.** CatholicMatch, Higher Bond, Ark and Christian Mingle all ask about faith. Only Muzz lets women keep their photos private until they choose. Showing photos only after both people say yes to the faith summary is a real difference.
4. **Every subscription found renews automatically, and renewals are a top complaint.** Prices for comparison:
   - Ark: £59.99 for 6 months, the same as Covenant's example price.
   - Shaadi.com: £99.99 for 6 months.
   - Hinge+: about £12.99 to £14.99 a month.
   - eharmony: up to £349.99.
   - Human matchmakers: about £3,000 to £10,000.

   So Covenant's price matches the market. What is new is a season that never renews.
5. **Paying to be seen is almost everywhere, Christian apps included.** Examples are SALT's "Highlight profile", Ark's boosts, Upward's Super Likes, Jdate's "Spotlight" and Lox Club's "Skip the wait".
6. **Apps with too few people struggle.** CatholicMatch is described as "nearly dead or full of bots" and rates 2.25 on Google Play. Meet & Right rates 2.7 on the App Store. Christian Connection's Android installs are falling. Only apps with millions of users rate well: Muzz 4.4, SALT 4.3.
7. **The market is unstable.**
   - Spark Networks, which owns Christian Mingle and Jdate, is in insolvency; its German company filed for US Chapter 15 on 31 Mar 2026.
   - EliteSingles closed with two days' notice.
   - Thursday, a London app open only on Thursdays, closed its app in January 2025.

   That leaves an opening for Covenant. It also shows that a fixed weekday isn't enough to stand out.
8. **ID checks are now the minimum.** Hinge made a face check compulsory from February 2026, with an age check in the UK. Shaadi.com asks for government ID, and Muzz checks every member with a selfie. No app checks Companions' ID as well, or has a private safety line the Companions can't see.

## Covenant's clearest true difference
People talk only with a verified Companion beside each of them. They start only after both have said yes to each other's faith answers, and before photos can shape the choice. A possible line, for the brand and pastoral reviewers to check: "Faith first. Faces second. Never alone."

## Risks
- **Apple guideline 4.3(b): high.** Apple tightened this rule on 9 Jun 2026. New dating apps must now be "meaningfully different", and Apple may remove stale ones. A marriage app for one community was rejected twice in September 2026, despite ID checks and daily limits.
- **Too few people at launch: high.** Covenant's pool is split by country, tradition, who people are open to and marital history, and capped at 3 introductions a week.
- **Price expectations: medium.** SALT and Muzz let matched people chat free, while Covenant asks for a season.
- **Companion friction: medium to high.** It isn't yet proven that people will accept a Companion.
- **The weekday rhythm wearing thin: medium.**
- **A false sense of safety: medium.** Scammers targeted a new Orthodox app straight away.
- **Family pressure.** On Shaadi.com, parents create 25–50% of accounts.

## Recommendations
**Must**
1. Write Apple review notes that lead with what is different, with demo accounts for two members and two Companions. Test the 4.3(b) risk early through TestFlight external testing, which Apple reviews, before spending on the launch. iPhone builds start no earlier than 15 Jan 2027.
2. Open area by area from a London waitlist, with minimum numbers of men and women in each tradition. Never show an empty week.
3. Keep the no-renewal season at about £59.99, and say clearly what it pays for.
4. Have a person review every profile's text and photos, not only the ID check.
5. Keep the private safety line and the rule that nobody acts for the member. Offer no caste, ethnicity or community filters (pastoral study; the solicitor to check).

**Should**
1. Decide on video calls with Companions present.
2. Add a joining pledge.
3. Block screenshots once photos are shared.
4. Hold church-hosted events while the waitlist is open.
5. On the store, lead with "Faith first. Faces second. Never alone." rather than "for marriage" alone.

**Could**
1. Have a person check each area's first introductions in the early beta.
2. Add a "first meeting plan" the member shares with their Companion.
3. Reach out to Orthodox and Catholic communities that today's apps serve poorly.
