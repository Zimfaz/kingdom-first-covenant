Every review answers these three questions first, with evidence from the app (screens, wording, flows) and from research:
1. **What is the purpose of the app?** State it in one sentence, then the job it does in a person's day and week. Name any gap between the stated purpose and what the screens actually push people to do.
2. **What benefit does a person get, even if it is never stated?** List the hidden or second-order benefits: identity, belonging, rest, structure, self-compassion, reduced decision fatigue, gratitude, meaning, a sense of being held. For each, say which feature delivers it and how strong the evidence is. Also name the unstated costs: time, guilt, comparison, data worries.
3. **How does the app create a feel-good factor?** Walk the emotional journey of a typical day (morning, midday, evening, Sunday, a hard day, a day missed) and say where people feel calm, competent, grateful, connected or proud, and why. Separate healthy feel-good (meaning, gratitude, gentle competence, belonging) from hollow or risky feel-good (variable rewards, counts, compulsion). Say where the feeling is missing or could turn into pressure.

Then give:
- **Risks:** anything that could create guilt, shame, anxiety, compulsive checking, comparison, perfectionism, spiritual pressure ("earning" closeness to God) or harm to vulnerable people. That includes people in grief, depression, pregnancy or loss, eating disorders, scrupulosity or OCD, and people new to faith. Each risk gets a severity.
- **Recommendations:** numbered, each marked Must (before the ~10 Dec store build), Should, or Later (January update), with effort (small, medium, large). Each says which behaviour or feeling it supports and why. Propose layout and wording only. Never change the logo, fonts or colours. Never add streaks, scores, leaderboards, badges, loss-aversion messages or false urgency. Faith wording and care wording go to the pastoral reviewer before use.
- **Questions for the owner:** real decisions only.
